# Build the Rennstern app around the camera, not inside it

The "Rennstern Firmware Edition" can become a working app, but not as firmware and not as something that pushes looks into the α7S II. The realistic design has two parts. The first is a free, offline phone web app (PWA) that holds the filmmaker look presets, a recording-screen checklist, a button reference, a slate/shot log and shot lists. The second is a set of local desktop scripts (ffmpeg, whisper.cpp, checksum offload) for post-production. You still dial the Picture Profile in on the camera, guided by the app. That last point is the hard limit: Sony's current Camera Remote SDK does not support the α7S II, and Sony has retired the legacy Wi-Fi API that the camera does speak. Neither that API nor USB/PTP control through libgphoto2 is documented to select Picture Profiles PP1–PP7 or to recall M1–M3 memory settings. Exposure settings, white balance and record start/stop are reachable remotely, but only with native or desktop code, never from a browser on iPhone. The money comes less from camera control (the α7S II costs almost nothing to control today) and more from replacing a slate app, transcription, review hosting and the NLE. With a free toolchain, a plausible paid stack of about €1,300–1,900/year falls by roughly €700–1,100/year, and the app itself costs €0–25 to build over a weekend to a few weeks. The savings figures are estimates built on assumptions, not survey data.

## The α7S II sits outside every modern Sony control path

Sony's current **Camera Remote SDK (Ver. 2.02.00, June 2026)** lists bodies from the α7S III / α7 IV / α1 generation onward, plus the FX and cinema lines. **The ILCE-7SM2 is not on that list** ([Sony SDK](https://support.d-imaging.sony.co.jp/app/sdk/en/index.html)). The SDK started in 2020 with only the α7R IV and α9 II ([SGP forum](https://forum.sequencegeneratorpro.com/t/sony-camera-remote-sdk-finally-available/11832)) and has never reached 2015-era bodies. What the α7S II does have is the older JSON-RPC-over-HTTP Camera Remote API, which works over a direct Wi-Fi link ([dltHub](https://dlthub.com/context/source/sony-camera-remote-api)). Sony replaced that API with the SDK and said provision of the old API "will be closed" ([DPReview](https://www.dpreview.com/news/0419921057/sony-releases-software-development-kit-sdk-for-remote-camera-access/)). Community wrappers still work, such as pysony, which includes an example that starts a video recording ([pysony](https://github.com/bloodevil/sony_camera_api)), and a .NET client with SSDP discovery ([twenzel](https://github.com/twenzel/SonyCameraRemoteControl)).

The API needs the on-camera Smart Remote Control (Embedded) app running. That app runs movie-mode remote control on "a7S and later" bodies ([Brian Smith](https://briansmith.com/sony-smart-remote-embbedded-app/)). However, **Sony ended PlayMemories Camera Apps downloads on 31 August 2025** ([Sony Asia](https://www.sony-asia.com/electronics/support/articles/00288923); [Sony USA](https://www.sony.com/electronics/support/articles/00289030)). If Smart Remote isn't already installed on Rennstern's body, the only way to get it now is through community sideloading of archived apps ([Reddit](https://www.reddit.com/r/SonyAlpha/comments/16pkdvu/how_to_archive_sony_playmemories_camera_apps/)). Sony's own Imaging Edge Mobile rates the α7S II "Compatible, but with restrictions" for remote shooting ([Sony IEM list](https://support.d-imaging.sony.co.jp/support/cscs/l/iemproducts/index.php)). Its newer bodies have moved on to Creators' App ([Imaging Edge](https://imagingedge.sony.net/l/ie-mobile.html)).

USB is the more promising transport. **libgphoto2 lists "Sony Alpha-A7S II (Control)" with image capture, trigger capture, liveview and configuration** ([gPhoto](http://www.gphoto.org/proj/libgphoto2/support.php)). The commercial app Shutter+ also gets exposure control, tap-to-focus, video recording and JPEG/video transfer working on this body over both Wi-Fi and USB ([Shutter+](https://shutter.dev/docs/sony-a7s-ii)). So third-party control is clearly possible. Still, one α7S II user could not set zoom or focus through gphoto2 ([gphoto2 #533](https://github.com/gphoto/gphoto2/issues/533)), and nobody has published a confirmed per-property list for this model.

## Look presets must be a guided checklist, because Picture Profiles stay on the body

The "premade filmmaker look presets" feature runs into the camera's limits. Public API wrappers and Sony-adjacent descriptions cover exposure, white balance, recording and liveview, and **none mention Picture Profile** ([pysony](https://github.com/bloodevil/sony_camera_api); [Brian Smith](https://briansmith.com/sony-smart-remote-embbedded-app/)). Smart Remote Embedded even keeps quality settings separate from the camera's own ([Brian Smith](https://briansmith.com/sony-smart-remote-embbedded-app/)). The conclusion that PP and memory recall can't be set remotely rests on their absence from the documentation, not on an explicit Sony statement. Confirm it in one afternoon by running `getAvailableApiList` and `gphoto2 --list-config` against the actual camera.

The only theoretical route into hidden settings is OpenMemories. Its Tweak tool edits the camera's Backup.bin (about 15,000 mostly undocumented entries) and can lift the 30-minute recording limit. However, it "can only change settings that are already there and just hidden by Sony" and has been unmaintained since 2017. It also carries the warning: "If you break your camera, you get to keep both pieces" ([OpenMemories-Tweak](https://github.com/ma1co/OpenMemories-Tweak)). Users report failures on some firmware versions ([DPReview](https://www.dpreview.com/forums/threads/open-memories-tweak-on-a7r2-firmware-v4-0.4168039/)). The research found no evidence that anyone has written Picture Profiles this way. For a working studio camera, that route is research-grade risk, not a product feature.

The practical preset design therefore follows the camera's limits. Each Rennstern look becomes a card that lists the PP slot and its gamma/colour mode/black level values to dial in by hand, and the matching LUT for post. If a native or desktop client is added later, the card can also push the exposure values (ISO, shutter, aperture, WB, exposure compensation) that the remote paths can reach. The "recording screen" and "button reference" are pure UI and fully buildable. In this design the app supplies the knowledge and the camera does the work.

## A free PWA plus local scripts beats native code on every axis

A browser can't drive the camera. **WebUSB works only in Chromium browsers and is unsupported in Safari on iOS** ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/WebUSB_API)). **iOS PWAs also lack Web Bluetooth, Web Serial and background processing** ([MagicBell](https://www.magicbell.com/blog/pwa-ios-limitations-safari-support-complete-guide)). An HTTPS page calling the camera's plain-HTTP Wi-Fi API would also run into mixed-content and CORS blocks. That rules out camera control from the PWA, but a PWA is still the right tool for presets, checklists, slate and shot lists: it works offline on any phone and can be hosted for free. AI builders help here too. Lovable has a free tier of 5 credits/day and Pro at $25/mo, Bolt Pro costs $25/mo, and Replit Core costs about $20–25/mo ([Creatr](https://getcreatr.com/ai-app-builder-pricing-comparison-2026)). One month of Pro is plenty. Real agent usage can push spend to $50–150/mo ([Vibe Coding Academy](https://www.vibecodingacademy.ai/blog/lovable-vs-bolt-vs-replit-comparison-2026)), so cancel once the MVP ships.

Native apps add cost and risk without unlocking Picture Profiles. Apple's developer program costs **$99/year** ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/programs/)), and Google Play is a $25 one-off ([SplitMetrics](https://splitmetrics.com/blog/google-play-apple-app-store-fees/)). If remote exposure and record control are wanted, a laptop script over gphoto2 or pysony is cheaper than a native app and reaches the same ceiling.

Post-production automation runs on desktop scripts. ffmpeg's `lut3d` filter batch-applies .cube LUTs for proxies and dailies ([Jeff Geerling](https://www.jeffgeerling.com/blog/2026/apply-lut-color-grade-with-ffmpeg/)). An old bug report notes slightly off output ([FFmpeg trac](https://trac.ffmpeg.org/ticket/2847)), so final grades belong in Resolve. whisper.cpp transcribes offline and is MIT-licensed ([GitHub](https://github.com/ggml-org/whisper.cpp)). Free offload verification already exists in Stow ([Newsshooter](https://www.newsshooter.com/2026/07/15/stow-free-copy-verification-software/)). One 2026 trap: **DaVinci Resolve 21.1 removed scripting from the free version**, so automating Resolve now requires Studio at $295 ([Puget Systems](https://www.pugetsystems.com/blog/2026/09/10/how-davinci-resolve-free-v21-1-scripting-changes-affect-puget-bench/)). Effort estimates are unsourced judgment: roughly 1–3 days for the PWA and 1–2 part-time weeks for the scripts. A native camera-control app would take weeks to months with an uncertain payoff.

| Component | Tool | Cost | Talks to α7S II? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Presets, recording screen, button reference, slate, shot list | PWA (AI builder or single-file app) | €0–25 | No |
| Remote exposure / record start-stop (optional) | Laptop script: gphoto2 (USB) or pysony (Wi-Fi + Smart Remote) | €0 | Partly; no PP/MR |
| LUT proxies, transcription, offload | ffmpeg, whisper.cpp, Stow | €0 | n/a |
| Resolve automation (optional) | Resolve Studio | $295 one-time | n/a |

## Savings come from the toolchain, not camera control

Camera control costs almost nothing to replace. Sony's Monitor & Control costs $4.99/mo ([Sony CCMC](https://www.sony.net/ccmc/)) and targets newer bodies. The α7S II relies on free Imaging Edge Mobile, and users already find Sony's remote apps unreliable ([Reddit](https://www.reddit.com/r/SonyAlpha/comments/1cgrs5q/is_it_worth_getting_the_sony_app_at_all_ios/)). The real targets are elsewhere. MovieSlate 8 costs **$24.99/mo or $299.99 one-time**, though its listed tiers are inconsistent ([Pro Filmmaker Apps](https://profilmmakerapps.com/app/movieslate/)). StudioBinder's free plan caps shot lists at 10 shots ([StudioBinder](https://support.studiobinder.com/en/articles/10106468-choosing-the-right-studiobinder-plan)). HappyScribe Pro costs $19/mo billed annually ([HappyScribe](https://www.happyscribe.com/pricing)). Frame.io Pro costs $15 per seat per month, and its free tier allows 2 GB ([Frame.io](https://frame.io/pricing)). Premiere costs US$22.99/mo ([Adobe](https://www.adobe.com/products/premiere/plans.html)), while Resolve is free ([Blackmagic](https://www.blackmagicdesign.com/products/davinciresolve)). Dehancer Studio costs $588/year ([Dehancer](https://www.dehancer.com/pricing)), but Dehancer also offers a free false-colour tool ([Dehancer shop](https://www.dehancer.com/shop)).

Against an assumed solo stack of about €1,300–1,900/year, the app's own features (slate, log, shot list, LUT cards) replace roughly €300–350/year. Switching to Resolve free, whisper.cpp and Frame.io Free or a self-hosted review link brings the total to about €700–1,100/year. Music licensing is the item that resists replacement. Epidemic Sound Pro costs $16.99/mo billed yearly ([Epidemic Sound](https://www.epidemicsound.com/pricing/)), and commercial client work needs a real licence. These totals depend on which subscriptions Rennstern actually pays for today, so run the numbers against the studio's real bills.

## Conclusion

The "Firmware Edition" name implies something the hardware won't allow. The defensible product is a field manual that runs on the phone, paired with a post-production pipeline on the laptop. The camera's own gaps make the app worth having: Sony stopped supporting this body, its app store is closed, and the Picture Profile values exist only in the filmmaker's head or notes. An app that captures them reliably protects a workflow Sony has abandoned. The first concrete step costs nothing. Plug the α7S II into a laptop, run `gphoto2 --list-config`, and check whether Smart Remote is installed. Those two checks settle whether remote exposure and record control ever belong in the roadmap. Avoid OpenMemories on the working body unless a spare camera is available to risk.
